What undisclosed automated callers cost in handle time

Nobody can currently count the automated callers that never say they are automated, so nobody can price them either. This page does the arithmetic from your assumptions instead: change any of the four numbers and every figure below changes with it.

Inputs

Your assumptions

01 / 04
calls

Every inbound call the line takes in a month, human and automated alike.

minutes

Time a caller spends holding, before and during the call.

%

Your assumption, not a finding. Nobody can currently count these callers, which is the gap the disclosure controls address.

$ / hour

Wages plus benefits, tooling and overhead, per hour of staffed time.

The four starting values are round placeholders, chosen to show the arithmetic working. They are not estimates of any real call centre, and this project has not surveyed one.

Method

How the figures are worked out

03 / 04

Four multiplications, in this order:

  1. Inbound calls a month × assumed undisclosed share = calls from undisclosed agents.
  2. Those calls × average hold time = hold minutes a month.
  3. Hold minutes ÷ 60 = staffed hours a month.
  4. Staffed hours × loaded cost of a staffed hour = handle-time cost a month; × 12 for the year.

The fractional call count is kept rather than rounded. A share of a volume is legitimately fractional, and rounding it to a whole call partway through would give the result a precision the assumption has not got.

The same arithmetic is implemented once, in src/impersonation_cost.py, and this page is generated from it, so the figures on screen are the figures its tests check.

Boundaries

What this does not show

04 / 04
  • It is not a saving. The hold time is spent whether or not the caller discloses itself. Disclosure changes who your staff know they are speaking to, and what the call is allowed to cover; it does not shorten the queue. Any figure here read as “what disclosure would save us” has been read wrong.
  • It is not a measurement. NHID-Clinical is an open research project and reference implementation. It has run no pilot on live payer traffic, so it has no observed share of undisclosed callers to offer you, and it does not supply one here.
  • It does not compare vendors. No product, platform or supplier is ranked, scored or named on this page.
  • It is not a return-on-investment case. There is no price, no payback period and no claim about what any control costs or returns.

What the project does have is the open control set and its conformance tests. The specification states the controls; the developer pages show the reference implementation; the shadow-evaluation guide is how an organisation can measure its own traffic rather than borrow an assumption from this page.
