# Voice-fraud statistics: what is sourced, and what is not usable

**Status:** evidence note · **Checked:** 2026-11-05 · CC BY 4.0

These figures reached the project through an AI search summary. Each was checked against
its primary source, or the closest one found.

- **Confirmed:** usable, quoted exactly as below, with the source and its date.
- **Corrected:** the circulating version was wrong. Only the corrected form may be used.
- **Not usable:** no primary source was found, or the sources disagree.

All are **third-party figures about the wider fraud landscape**. None measures
NHID-Clinical, a payer's phone lines, or AI callers requesting PHI. Quote them only as
context, never as evidence of what NHID-Clinical does
([`../claim-boundaries.md`](../claim-boundaries.md)).

| Claim as circulated | Verdict | Use exactly this | Source |
| :-- | :-- | :-- | :-- |
| AI-generated voices on calls are "artificial" under the TCPA | **Confirmed** | "In February 2024 the FCC ruled that calls using AI-generated voices are 'artificial' under the TCPA, so they need the called party's prior express consent." It is not a ban, and it is about outbound calls to consumers, not payer lines. | FCC Declaratory Ruling FCC 24-17, CG Docket 23-362, adopted 8 Feb 2024 ([FCC release](https://www.fcc.gov/document/fcc-makes-ai-generated-voices-robocalls-illegal)) |
| Synthetic-voice fraud attacks on insurers up 475% | **Confirmed, vendor-reported** | "Pindrop reported a 475% increase in synthetic voice fraud attacks on insurance companies in 2024, from its analysis of more than 1.2 billion customer calls." | Pindrop 2025 Voice Intelligence & Security Report, as reported by [Fierce Healthcare](https://www.fiercehealthcare.com/payers/insurance-fraud-increased-19-synthetic-voice-attacks-2024) and [BenefitsPRO](https://www.benefitspro.com/2025/06/23/insurance-companies-experienced-an-increase-in-ai-driven-fraud-attacks-in-2024) |
| Deepfake activity up 680% year over year | **Not usable** | The same report's coverage leads with other numbers: "1,300%" in press releases, and "1,337%" in a Pindrop case study. Do not quote any of the three until the report itself is read and one figure is pinned to its definition. | Pindrop site and press coverage ([SpeechTech](https://www.speechtechmag.com/Articles/News/Speech-Technology-News/Voice-Deepfake-Fraud-Surged-1300-Percent-170061.aspx)) |
| 1 in 4 adults experienced, or know someone who experienced, an AI voice-cloning scam | **Confirmed, dated** | "In McAfee's 2023 survey of about 7,000 adults in seven countries, one in four said they had experienced an AI voice-cloning scam or knew someone who had." | McAfee, *The Artificial Imposter*, May 2023 ([McAfee blog](https://www.mcafee.com/blogs/privacy-identity-protection/artificial-imposters-cybercriminals-turn-to-ai-voice-cloning-for-a-new-breed-of-scam/)) |
| 77% of those targeted lost money | **Corrected** | "Of the one in ten respondents who said they received a message from an AI voice clone, 77% said they lost money." The base is the 1 in 10 who were targeted, not the 1 in 4. | McAfee, May 2023 (same source) |
| A voice can be cloned from 3 seconds of audio | **Confirmed, vendor claim** | "McAfee researchers reported that three seconds of audio was enough to produce a clone." It is a vendor test result from 2023, not a peer-reviewed measurement. | McAfee, May 2023 (same source) |
| Cloning gives an 85–99% acoustic match | **Not usable** | No primary source was found for the range. | — |
| Humans spot synthetic voices only about 60% of the time | **Corrected** | "In a 2023 peer-reviewed study (PLOS ONE, 529 listeners), people identified deepfake speech 73% of the time, and training helped only slightly." The circulated "60%" and its attribution to UC Berkeley were not found. Separate studies report lower accuracy on cloned voices, but their exact figures were not checked here. | Mai et al., UCL, PLOS ONE, Aug 2023 ([ScienceDaily summary](https://sciencedaily.com/releases/2023/08/230802162437.htm)) |

## Before using any of these externally

- Read the primary document, not the summary linked here. Record the page or table the
  number comes from.
- Keep the date and population with the number ("in 2024", "in a 2023 survey of
  7,000 adults").
- Never place these figures next to an NHID-Clinical measurement in a way that implies
  NHID-Clinical caught, prevented or measured them.
