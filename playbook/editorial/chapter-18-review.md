# Editorial Review — Chapter 18: Industry Adoption

*Review pass following the first complete draft.*

## Overall assessment

The chapter carries Part V's hardest integrity burden — writing about
adoption dynamics that have not happened — and handles it with the
discipline the earlier reviews demanded: the opening scenario is
labeled composite before its first line, the examples are labeled
individually, and the maturity ground truth (partners being sought,
no production deployments) is restated in the executive summary
before any dynamic is projected. The chapter's analytical core —
unilateral value first, network effects second, infrastructure last —
is the correct and honest theory of voluntary-baseline adoption, and
"identical questions, not coordinated action" is the mechanism stated
more clearly than the framework's own materials state it. Treating
provider key custody as the honest bottleneck (rather than a detail)
is the chapter's most responsible analytical choice.

## Critique

1. **The conference scenario's closing line slightly romanticizes**
   ("multiplied, is what industry adoption... actually looks like").
   For a projected dynamic, "actually" overreaches; "would look
   like" is the honest mood. Queued — integrity class, small but
   the chapter's frame depends on it.
2. **The RFP line-item example quotes model language.** Useful and
   extraction-worthy — but model procurement language drifts toward
   being read as endorsement-required text. Add the Chapter 6
   disclosure-wording caveat pattern: one conformant form, not
   required words. Queued.
3. **The custodial example implies an insurance answer to the
   liability question** ("the same professional-services insurance").
   That is a plausible pattern, not settled practice, and liability
   is counsel territory — soften to "addresses the liability
   question through its existing professional-services framework"
   and let the front-matter disclaimer carry the rest. Queued.
4. **The stall-risk list is the chapter's best paragraph** and is
   buried mid-sequence. Promotion candidate at revision — possibly
   to a figure callout, since Figure 18-3 already carries the
   risks.

## Weak arguments identified

- **"Procurement documents are the industry's real standards
  bodies"** — aphoristic overstatement with a real point inside it.
  Keep the point (procurement propagates expectations faster than
  standards processes), lose the absolutism. Queued.
- **The free-option framing** ("as close to a free option as
  governance investment offers") prices the downside as bounded but
  ignores opportunity cost and the custody burden the chapter
  itself documents for providers. Scope the claim to
  payers/vendors, whose on-ramps genuinely are low-cost. Queued.

## Transitions

- **In:** Part V opens at ecosystem altitude with the maturity
  ground-truth restated — per the Part IV → V brief. Verified.
- **Out:** the regulatory-window argument hands Chapter 19 its
  frame (mandates codify experience or improvisation). Chapter 19's
  debts: the full driver mapping with the mapped-not-certified
  discipline, the NIST public-comment characterization (comment,
  not endorsement — the framework's own materials are emphatic),
  and the R4-base-only scope claim's regulatory reading.

## Diagram recommendations

## Verdict

Sound; the projection discipline holds. Five small queued edits, all
tone-or-scope. The book's future-facing material is now anchored to
structure rather than hope, which is what Part V required.
