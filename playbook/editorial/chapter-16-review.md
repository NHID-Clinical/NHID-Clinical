# Editorial Review — Chapter 16: Risk Management

*Review pass following the first complete draft.*

## Overall assessment

The four-column structure (mitigated / transformed / residual /
introduced) is the correct genre translation and clears all four debts
registered against the chapter: the incidence argument (Chapter 2)
lands as the register-justification line; the non-blocking
detection-latency trade (Chapter 7), the custody/revocation risks
(Chapter 11), and the human-social-engineer residual (Chapter 3) all
appear in their proper columns. The chapter's best insight is
structural: every *introduced* risk arrived with its treatment already
specified by an earlier chapter — which retroactively reveals the
book's implementation chapters as having been writing the risk
register all along. "The residual that got budget" makes the honesty
posture commercially legible, which Part IV needed.

## Critique

1. **The transformed column is the thinnest of the four** — three
   arrows, each one sentence. It is also the chapter's most original
   framing. Consider one more paragraph on
   detection→verification's cost asymmetry (flat-cost checking vs.
   arms race, Chapter 11's economics) *in risk terms* — annualized
   effort, predictability. Queued as an expansion candidate, the
   book's first.
2. **The introduced-risk example ("the introduced risk that fired")
   narrates a contained incident with tidy instrumentation.** The
   composite convention covers it, but the counterfactual comparison
   ("unbounded in scope and duration") repeats Chapter 11's
   compromised-middle overstatement — the same softening applies
   (per-payer rituals still existed). Align both passages at the
   consistency pass. Queued.
3. **The register-entry framing advice ("title it by the exposure,
   not the technology") is excellent and buried** mid-section.
   Promotion candidate for the executive summary.
4. **TPRM section is compact and correct** — the two standardized
   assessment questions (per-tenant keys, declared chains) are
   genuinely portable artifacts. No change.

## Weak arguments identified

- **"Residual honesty earns durable sponsorship"** is argued by one
  composite example. The mechanism (stated limits survive scrutiny;
  overclaims don't) is sound and consistent with the book's spine,
  but the example does heavy lifting. Acceptable; do not add a second
  example — the point is made.
- **The claim that the register entry "will not be managed by anyone
  else's program"** is strong but correct as stated: the incidence
  argument supports it (no other party bears the cost), and
  regulators (Chapter 19) may eventually force management — the
  sentence says *anyone else's program*, not *ever*. Holds.

## Transitions

- **In:** the CISO's missing-entry scenario picks up the metrics
  one-pager from Chapter 15 directly — the tightest chapter-to-chapter
  handoff in Part IV. Verified.
- **Out:** hands Chapter 17 the "owners and review dates" thread —
  the register names owners; governance is where owners get their
  authority. Chapter 17's debts: the sidebar decision on
  receiving-side doctrine (Chapters 6/10), policy-version governance
  (Chapters 7/10), the review SOP as standing operation (Chapter 7),
  and the change process for framework upgrades (Chapter 5's
  adopt-by-version).
