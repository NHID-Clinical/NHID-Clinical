# Editorial Review — Chapter 17: Governance

*Review pass following the first complete draft.*

## Overall assessment

The champion-leaves scenario is the correct frame for a governance
chapter — it converts an abstract subject into a testable property
(does the program survive its builder?) and the forked outcome
structure earns the chapter's closing line about succession as audit.
The chapter also performs the book's largest outstanding
consolidation: the receiving-side conduct doctrine, built piecemeal
across Chapters 1, 6, and 10, now has one named, owned policy — the
sidebar question from Chapter 10's review is resolved (policy, not
sidebar, was the right answer). Version governance is the strongest
section: "adopting by pointer is ungovernable" pays off Chapter 5,
and the upgrade-lane worked example (re-baseline before the dashboard
moves) is the chapter's most practically valuable content.

## Critique

1. **Typo: "unggoverned"** in the tuning example — caught in review
   and corrected in place. (Recorded because this review is the
   chapter's only quality gate until the consistency pass.)
2. **The committee's not-owned list is as important as its owned
   list and gets one sentence.** The governs-vs-operates boundary
   deserves a second beat — committees that operate are the failure
   mode most readers will actually have seen. Minor expansion
   queued.
3. **"Boundary-language register" is a new coinage** for a practice
   the book has used since Chapter 5. Good instrument, but the
   consistency pass should back-reference it (Chapters 5, 9, 14
   each planted boundary sentences) so it reads as consolidation,
   not novelty.
4. **The successor example's "extraction cards pinned above the
   desk"** is charming but confirms the front-matter obligation:
   the book now references its extraction-card genre as a practice;
   the front matter must list the cards (current count: ten
   candidates through Chapter 17). **Front-matter item, third
   confirmation.**

## Weak arguments identified

- **"Governance encoded in the change pipeline is real"** — the
  implementation guidance's strongest claim, and correct, but the
  chapter should not imply charter documents are worthless — they
  are the authority the pipeline gates *implement*. The draft's
  "advisory" framing is acceptable; no change, noted only to
  prevent hardening at revision.
- **The upgrade example assumes scoring changes are detectable by
  re-baseline** — true when the parity/re-baseline discipline runs,
  which is the point, but circular if quoted alone. The example
  depends on its process; the caption of Figure 17-2 should carry
  that dependency.
