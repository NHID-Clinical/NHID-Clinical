# Editorial Review — Chapter 13: Integration with Existing Healthcare Systems

*Review pass following the first complete draft.*

## Overall assessment

The chapter holds to its brief from Chapter 12's review — breadth with
pointers, no new depth — and finds its own thesis in doing so: seams
have owners, and integration succeeds by meeting owners inside their
own systems. The engineer's-week scenario is the right shape (the code
was the easy week), and the technical/organizational estimate inversion
is the chapter's genuinely useful executive insight. The three-stage
contract progression (conduct → evidence → thresholds) is the book's
best procurement contribution and correctly sequences against the
pilot/baseline dependency built in Chapters 9 and 15.

## Critique

1. **The already-conformant vendor example is aspirational.** No vendor
   is known to have built against the public routes for its own QA;
   the example narrates the *possibility* the open API creates.
   Standing integrity rule: mark as the dynamic the public routes make
   possible. Queued — integrity class, same family as Chapters 4 and
   10.
2. **The BAA paragraph walks the legal line carefully but cites the
   template as "a starting artifact"** — verify at copyedit that the
   framework's BAA template is characterized accurately (it exists in
   the materials) and that the book's no-legal-advice disclaimer
   (front-matter item from Chapter 5) covers this section explicitly.
3. **The audit-store-that-wasn't example is the book's most honest
   mid-size-organization moment** — keep it. Its debt-ticket
   resolution models the imperfect-but-recorded posture the book has
   been teaching. No change.
4. **Chapter is lighter on operational texture than its Part III
   siblings** — appropriate for a connective chapter, but the seam
   map must carry real weight as the master figure; if Figure 13-1
   underdelivers, the chapter has no spine. Commission note added.

## Weak arguments identified

- **"Technical seams are smaller than expected" generalizes from this
  framework's genuinely small footprint.** True here (one webhook
  forward is Tier 1), but stated almost as a law of integration.
  Scope the claim to this framework. Queued.
- **The renewal example's "one redline round"** — invented precision
  again, minor. Generalize to "minimal negotiation." Queued.

## Transitions

- **In:** opens from the ticket Part III built toward; inventory-first
  per Chapter 8's discipline. Verified.
- **Out:** Part III → Part IV seam. Chapter 14 inherits: the pilot
  narrative debt from Chapter 5's review (worked composite, labeled),
  the good-enough bars from Chapter 9, and the
  measurements-not-certifications rule. Its brief is now the fullest
  in the book.

## Diagram recommendations

- **Figure 13-1 (seam map)** — master figure; owners as roles, not
  names; governing-chapter references in the margin. Extraction

- **Figure 13-2** must quote the guide's own effort numbers, not

## Verdict

Sound; completes Part III as connective tissue should — by making the
next part's work visible. Two integrity-class labels queued (vendor
example, redline precision), one scoping edit. Implementation is now
fully specified from measurement through paper; Part IV can argue
adoption at organizational scale.
