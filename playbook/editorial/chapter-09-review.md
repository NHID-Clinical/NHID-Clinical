# Editorial Review — Chapter 9: Shadow Evaluations

*Review pass following the first complete draft.*

## Overall assessment

The chapter is the book's most direct transcription of an existing artifact
(the pilot kit), and it survives the transcription risk the same way
Chapter 6 did — by adding judgment the source doesn't carry: the political
reading of observe-only design ("no enemies made"), the
implausible-numbers-are-mapping-bugs heuristic, the charter-and-calendar
governance mechanics. The opening meeting is the right scenario — it
dramatizes the pilot's actual innovation, which is organizational (nothing
promised, no permission needed) rather than technical.

## Critique

1. **Two examples are composites and are labeled with varying clarity.**
   The workflow contrast opens with "a composite of the pattern the kit is
   built to surface" — correct labeling. The null-result example carries no
   label and reads as a case. Per the standing integrity rule (Chapters 4,
   7), add the composite marker. **Queued — integrity class.** The
   mapping-failure example is fine: it narrates the kit's own documented
   trap.
2. **The 9% figure in the null-result example is invented.** Composite
   examples should avoid false-precision numbers; "roughly one in ten"
   carries the point without manufacturing a statistic. Queued.
3. **The chapter's guidance occasionally exceeds the kit.** "Schedule the
   decision meeting when you charter" and "keep vendor lists out of
   headlines" are the author's operational judgment, not kit content. This
   is the book's value-add and is fine — but the consistency pass should
   confirm such advice is never attributed to the framework's materials.
4. **Repetition of the honest-boundary material** (third appearance after
   Chapters 4 and 6). Deliberate and defensible — it appears here in
   pilot-caveat form, which is where readers will actually copy it from —
   but Figure 9-4's caption should acknowledge the repetition ("restated
   from Chapter 4 for pilot use") rather than presenting as new.

## Weak arguments identified

- **"Information payoff this certain"** — the null result is framed as
  certain value, but organizations with genuinely low automation exposure
  get modest value for the effort. The asymmetry argument survives
  (bounded cost either way); the adjective could soften to "unusually
  favorable." Minor.
- **The claim that threshold-setting without a baseline "loses"** is
  asserted from negotiation logic, not evidence. It is the right advice;
  frame as reasoning ("negotiate against your own data or against vendor
  pushback — those are the options"), which the draft nearly does.

## Transitions

- **In:** Part III opens mechanics-first as Chapter 8's review requested;
  the concepts arrive pre-defined. Verified.
- **Out:** the handoff to Chapter 10 ("what changes when you stop only
  watching") is clean and sets up the disconnect-on-detect retirement
  promised since Chapter 1. Chapter 10 now owes: the graduated ladder,
  the deterministic decision path as enforcement due process, and the
  Chapter 4 note about receiving-side policy not punishing disclosure.

## Diagram recommendations

- **Figure 9-3 (good-enough card)** joins the extraction-artifact genre —
  front matter's list grows to three (4-4, 5-1, 9-3).
- **Figure 9-1** must keep the your-systems/kit-components dotted line;
  it is the no-production-risk claim in visual form.
- **Figure 9-2's** two decision diamonds are the pilot's governance —
  do not let a designer render it as a plain Gantt bar.

## Verdict

Sound. Two integrity-class fixes queued (composite label, invented
precision), both one-line. The chapter completes the measurement arc begun
in Chapter 4; enforcement can now be argued from data.
