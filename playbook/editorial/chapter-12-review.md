# Editorial Review — Chapter 12: Audit Trails

*Review pass following the first complete draft.*

## Overall assessment

The chapter completes the book's longest arc — the Chapter 3 dispute,
re-run and resolved in an afternoon — and the resolution is written with
the right restraint: the dispute doesn't vindicate anyone; it *informs*
everyone, which keeps the coordination-failure frame intact even at the
moment of triumph. "Retain evidence, not conclusions" is the chapter's
contribution to the extraction-artifact genre, and "audit changes who
can ask questions" is the institutional insight that lifts the chapter
above record-keeping mechanics. Outcome-code semantics (capture
deficiency vs. conduct violation) connect beautifully back to Chapter
7's false-accusation economics — the book's threads are visibly
converging.

## Critique

1. **The opening resolves generously.** Three delegated calls, two
   honest-but-passportless ones, everyone learns something — no bad
   actor at all. Realistic disputes sometimes end with a breach. The
   choice is defensible (the book has argued since Chapter 2 that the
   common case is coordination failure, and a breach ending would
   re-dramatize the adversarial frame), but one clause acknowledging
   the other ending ("had the fifth call carried neither passport nor
   disclosure, this same evidence set is what a breach investigation
   starts from") would inoculate against the tidiness objection.
   Queued.
2. **The quarterly production drill and the four indexes are the
   author's operational synthesis**, not framework content — same
   attribution class as Chapter 10's ladder. The consistency pass
   should sweep all such synthesis for consistent framing; a
   book-level convention ("framework mechanisms vs. this book's
   recommended practice") is now overdue and should be stated in the
   front matter. **Front-matter action item (second).**
3. **The vendor-debugging example attributes a specific regression**
   ("a prompt change moved disclosure two turns later") to a composite
   vendor. Plausible mechanism, invented specifics — standing rule
   applies; label or generalize. Queued — integrity class.
4. **OpenTelemetry is deferred again** (flagged in Chapter 8's review,
   deferred here to Chapter 15). Chapter 15 now must actually deliver
   the observability thread or the book will have pointed at it three
   times and landed it nowhere. Added to Chapter 15's brief in bold.

## Weak arguments identified

- **"Cheap questions get asked"** — the argument that queryable audit
  changes organizational behavior is asserted from general
  data-analytics experience. It is the kind of claim readers will
  accept, but it predicts culture, not mechanics. The subjunctive
  framing ("the audit layer's quiet product") keeps it honest.
  Acceptable.
- **The afternoon timeline** depends on every prior chapter's
  discipline having been executed — indexes built, snapshots stored,
  bundles emitted. The chapter says this ("decisions made months
  before"), but the Figure 12-4 caption should carry it too, or the
  before/after image oversells.

## Transitions

- **In:** opens holding Chapter 11's retention preview, as designed.
  Verified.
- **Out:** hands Chapter 13 the seams (enrollment systems, audit
  stores, platforms, contracts). Chapter 13 is the last technical
  chapter; its brief is breadth with pointers, not new depth.

## Diagram recommendations

- **Figure 12-4 (the two disputes)** is the book's single best
  before/after candidate and belongs in any executive presentation of
  the book; caption must carry the executed-discipline caveat above.
- **Figure 12-2** is operationally the most valuable — it prevents

- **Figure 12-3** joins the extraction genre (fifth card).

## Verdict

Sound. One integrity-class fix (vendor example), one inoculation
clause, and the front-matter convention action item now carries two
entries. Part III's evidence chain — measure, enforce, authorize,
prove — is complete; Chapter 13 closes the part with integration
breadth.
