NHID-Clinical, as a doctor's office front desk

A representative walkthrough of the four behavioral controls (v1.3) and the cryptographic identity layer (NHID-Auth v2), staged as the thing almost everyone has actually lived through: checking in at a clinic. Hover any booth for its live rule-engine config.
Picture an AI voice agent calling a healthcare payer the same way you'd walk up to a clinic's front desk. Before the receptionist (the payer) hands over anything from a chart, a few things have to happen in order: you say who/what you are, you clear a privacy check before any record comes out, you're being watched for dishonesty the whole time, and there's always a button to ask for an actual human. That sequence — disclose → consent → behave → escalate — is exactly what IDG-01, PDX-01, DBC-01, and EIT-01 enforce on a real call. The badge floating above the caller the whole time is NHID-Auth v2 — a provider-signed, NPI-bound credential proving this caller is actually authorized to act for that doctor's office, like a verified staff ID.
