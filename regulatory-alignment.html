Where the rows come from: CMS-0057-F — FHIR API for PA transactions (ATR-01, FHIR R4 AuditEvent), AI transparency in decisions (IDG-01), human review availability (EIT-01). MACPAC May 2026 — AI transparency in prior auth and a human review pathway (IDG-01, EIT-01, ATR-01). DOJ FCA 2026 — explainability and an audit trail for AI-assisted billing (ATR-01, with the conformance test suite). State AI laws — inspectable, auditable AI decisions (IDG-01, DBC-01). NIST AI RMF — MEASURE 2.8 Direct for IDG-01, DBC-01, EIT-01, ATR-01; MAP 4.2 Partial for all five plus opt-in DLG-01; MEASURE 2.7 Partial for DLG-01.

The NIST CAISI request for information on AI agent security (docket NIST-2025-0035) is answered by NHID-Auth v2 rather than by a v1.3 control; NIST-2025-0035-0026 is a public comment, not an endorsement. STIR/SHAKEN is a carrier-layer attestation that a number is legitimate, not a control mapping; see below. FHIR output is validated against the R4 base specification (4.0.1) only; no Implementation Guide conformance is claimed.

Audit

FHIR R4 AuditEvent emission

One AuditEvent per conformance milestone, validated in CI.

02 / 06

Every NHID-Clinical conformance test suite (CTS) execution produces a HL7 FHIR R4 Bundle of AuditEvent resources — one per conformance milestone. These Bundles are validated against the FHIR R4 base specification (version 4.0.1) in CI before every merge.

AWhat is emitted per call

  1. nhid-session-startCall session initialized (DCM Application Activity).
  2. nhid-identity-disclosureIDG-01 evaluated; outcome reflects pass or fail (DCM Security Alert).
  3. nhid-auth-verificationProvider authorization recorded, when present (DCM User Authentication).
  4. nhid-phi-gatePDX-01 decision (DCM Security Alert).
  5. nhid-phi-exchangePHI exchange begins, when the gate cleared (DCM Patient Record).
  6. nhid-escalationEIT-01 escalation event, when triggered (DCM Application Activity).
  7. nhid-call-endSession ends; outcome reflects the aggregate decision (DCM Application Activity).

ANIST AI RMF 1.0 — subcategory crosswalk (mapped, not certified)

The strongest rows of a subcategory-level crosswalk against the official NIST AI RMF Playbook. Each row is evidence toward an outcome that belongs to the deploying organization, for the voice-call channel only — not a compliance determination or certification. Direct: the control's output is evidence of the outcome itself. Partial: it covers part of the outcome; the rest is the deployer's. Most of the 72 subcategories are organizational and stay with the deployer.

Regulatory pressure

Different jurisdictions are approaching synthetic voice from different directions

These are separate instruments with separate scopes, triggers and legal mechanisms. They do not add up to one healthcare AI voice rule, and none of them requires NHID-Clinical. What they share is a direction of travel: when a person is dealing with an automated system, they are increasingly entitled to know.

06 / 06
Regulatory reference points, what each establishes, and what it does not
InstrumentIn forceWhat it establishesWhat it does not
California AB 2905
Cal. Pub. Util. Code §2874
Accessed 25 Oct 2026		 1 Jan 2025 Covered automatic dialing-announcing devices must tell the called person when a prerecorded message uses an artificial voice. Does not govern every conversational AI call, and is not healthcare-specific.
FCC 24‑17
TCPA declaratory ruling
Accessed 25 Oct 2026		 8 Feb 2024 AI-generated voices fall within the TCPA's existing restrictions on artificial or prerecorded voice calls. Does not create a disclosure standard for provider–payer administrative calls.
EU AI Act Art. 50(1)
Disclosure of AI interaction
Accessed 25 Oct 2026		 2 Aug 2026 People must be informed when they are interacting directly with an AI system, unless that is obvious in the circumstances. Does not specify how disclosure is verified, scoped, or evidenced on a call. Article 50(2), on machine-readable marking of synthetic content, is a separate obligation on a separate timetable and is not what NHID-Clinical addresses.

● NHID-Clinical addresses the operational control point these instruments leave open: establishing identity, disclosure, authorization and evidence before sensitive workflow actions occur. It does not make any system legally compliant, and nothing here is legal advice.
